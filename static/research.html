The research behind the model

Two papers frame this project: a review of how machine learning speeds up perovskite discovery, and an experiment that took an ML prediction all the way to a working fuel cell cathode.

Accelerating perovskite materials discovery and correlated energy applications through artificial intelligence

Liang et al., Energy Materials 2022;2:200016 (review)
  • Lays out the ML workflow for perovskites: data preparation, feature engineering, model selection, evaluation, application.
  • Surveys open databases (Materials Project, OQMD, CMR, ICSD and others) and the need for high-quality data.
  • Covers interpretable models (tree models, SHAP, CGCNN) and why interpretation should agree with physics and chemistry.
  • Reports ML stability predictions for perovskite oxides, including candidate SOFC cathodes such as BaFe0.25V0.75O3 and La0.5Y0.5Co0.5Mn0.5O3.
  • Names the open challenges: small datasets, black-box models, and the gap between prediction and synthesis.

Machine learning-accelerated discovery of novel high-entropy spinel oxide cathodes for solid oxide fuel cells

Ma et al., Fuel 404 (2026) 136190
  • Trains XGBoost, Random Forest and Naive Bayes on 66 solid-state-reaction samples with 10 features to predict single-phase formation; ADASYN balances the classes.
  • XGBoost reaches 88% test accuracy and AUC 0.974; SHAP points to precursor volume, electronegativity and density deviations as key drivers.
  • Of five ML-selected compositions, two form single-phase spinels and are compatible with YSZ and GDC electrolytes.
  • Mn0.6Fe0.6Co0.6Cu0.6Mg0.6O4 reaches a polarization resistance of 0.87 Ω·cm² and a peak power density of 627 mW·cm-2 at 800 °C.
